Judgement in the Loop

Almost anything can be built now, and a surprising amount of it can be built by the end of the week. When making things gets this cheap, the interesting work moves somewhere else: deciding what is worth making, how much of it to hand to a machine, and what we would rather keep doing ourselves.

This guide is about that part of the job. It’s written for product people, and for anyone who has sat in a meeting where someone said “we could just use AI for that” and wasn’t quite sure whether they were right.

How each chapter works

Every chapter starts from a question you will probably meet at work sooner or later. It explains just enough of the technology for that question to make sense, spends most of its time on how to decide, and then looks at the whole thing the way a product person would.

  1. first

    What it is

    Enough to follow the conversation with your engineers, in plain words, with links out for anyone who wants the deep end.

  2. then

    How to decide

    The questions worth asking before you choose, and the signs that tell you it’s time to change your mind.

  3. and

    Through a product lens

    What it means for discovery, for delivery, and for how you’ll know whether any of it worked.

Each one closes with a short note on what should stay in human hands. That question runs underneath all the others, and the last chapter takes it on directly.
